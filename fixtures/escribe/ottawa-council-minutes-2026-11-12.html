14.4
Maple Grove Health Hub Request for Proposals

 
1.Coverpage - Maple Grove Health Hub Request for Proposals.pdf
2.Report - Maple Grove Health Hub Request for Proposals.pdf

File No. ACS2026-SI-HSI-0016 – Stittsville (Ward 6)

  • Committee Recommendation(s), as amended

    That Council:

    1. Receive the report respecting RFP No. 39126-97173-P01 for 1655 Maple Grove Road;
    2. Direct staff to review the results of RFP No. 39126-97173-P01 and identify modifications to the procurement and proposed transaction structure that could increase market interest and the likelihood of achieving the development of a primary care health facility, while ensuring that the transaction does not result in a net cost to the City;
    3. Direct staff to issue a new Request for Proposals for the development of a primary care facility or practice at 1655 Maple Grove Road, informed by the review undertaken pursuant to Recommendation 2, and that, in developing the RFP and proposed transaction structure, staff:
      1. Consider the feasibility and appropriateness of a Municipal Capital Facilities Agreement pursuant to section 110 of the Municipal Act, 2001, together with any other available legal or financial mechanisms that could facilitate development of the primary care facility while protecting the City's financial and legal interests, and give additional consideration to proposals that offer opportunities to advance other City of Ottawa strategic priorities, where compatible with the primary health care objectives of the project;
      2. Identify any amendments to the Zoning By-law required to permit the intended health hub use on the identified parcel, and provide that any City costs associated with the severance and rezoning of the lands be recovered through the transaction;
      3. Establish that the lands will be sold on an “as-is” basis. Where feasible, retain sufficient flexibility to consider appropriate cost-sharing arrangements where doing so would improve the efficiency or cost-effectiveness of servicing the remaining City-owned lands or otherwise advance City priorities;
      4. Consider transaction and development approaches that could improve project viability and encourage market participation, including phased or incremental development, shared servicing arrangements, deferred payment structures, or other appropriate mechanisms, while managing financial and legal risk to the City;
      5. Include, as appropriate, restrictive covenants, performance milestones, reversionary rights or other contractual or legal protections to provide reasonable assurance that the lands are developed and continue to be used primarily for the delivery of community-based primary health care services;
      6. Establish evaluation criteria that prioritize the delivery of physician-led community-based primary care services, preferably through a Family Health Organization (FHO), supported by allied health services such as nurse practitioners, registered nurses, social workers, and other interprofessional clinicians; and evaluating the anticipated number of net-new patients to be attached, the proposed timeline for achieving the attachment, and the proponents’ ability to demonstrate, sustain, and expand the proposed service capacity. The evaluation criteria should establish a minimum attachment capacity appropriate to the proposed clinician complement and model of care, with a target of 5,000 net-new patient attachments supported by the proposal;
      7. Establish a preferred parcel size of 4 acres for the proposed health facility, while allowing flexibility to consider a smaller parcel where sufficient to accommodate the proposed primary care services. A larger parcel of up to 8 acres may only be considered where the proponent demonstrates that the additional land is reasonably required to accommodate complementary medical, health-related or other ancillary uses that directly support or are associated with the delivery of primary care services, or to accommodate affordable housing uses, having regard to the efficient use of City-owned lands;
    4. Approve that staff report back to the Finance and Corporate Services Committee and Council in April 2027 with a progress update on the implementation of Recommendations 2 and 3.
    Carried

22.
Notices of Motion (for Consideration at Subsequent Meeting)

 

22.1
Advocating for eliminating twice yearly time changes

 
  • Moved byS. Menard
    Seconded byM. Carr

    WHEREAS, in 2026, the provincial governments of British Columbia, Alberta and Manitoba as well as the territorial government of the Northwest Territories have all discontinued or are in the process of discontinuing the practice of switching between daylight and standard time; and,

    WHEREAS the government of Saskatchewan in 1966 and the government of the Yukon in 2020 have implemented similar changes; and

    WHEREAS the newly formed provincial government of Québec has also recently announced its intent to discontinue the practice of switching between daylight and standard time in that province; and,

    WHEREAS, if the province of Québec implements this change, there are concerns of significant Impacts to residents of the National Capital Region, many of whom regularly cross the provincial boarder between Ontario and Québec, if changes are implemented on the Gatineau side but not the Ottawa side; and,

    WHEREAS in, light of Manitoba’s recent decision to switch to permanent Daylight Savings Time (DST), the government of Ontario announced on October 13, 2026, that it intends to make daylight saving time permanent in Kenora and the northwestern regions of Ontario that have historically been aligned with neighbouring Manitoba; 

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that Council write to the government of Ontario and to local MPPs to encourage them to support the elimination of twice-yearly time changes throughout Ontario, so that the whole province is consistent and aligned with the neighbouring provinces of Manitoba and Québec.

22.2
Carlington West Gateway Speed Measure

 
  • Moved byR. Brockington
    Seconded byR. King

    WHEREAS the Carlington neighbourhood of River Ward currently has a default speed limit of 40 km/h on residential streets, established as part of the Entry Gateway Speed Limit (traffic calming) initiative; 

    WHEREAS the Carlington Community Association Transportation Committee has worked closely with the local Councillor in recent years to identify, pursue, and implement various traffic calming initiatives within the neighbourhood and the Carlington Community Association is supportive of this motion; 

    WHEREAS Council approved Motion ACS2025-OCC-CCS-0080 on July 23, 2025, which reduced the speed limit from 40 km/h to 30 km/h on residential streets within Carlington East, bounded by Carling Avenue, Fisher Avenue, Kingston Avenue, and Merivale Road (excluding arterial roads), and there is now a strong local desire to complete the phased-in approach by amending the speed limit on local residential streets from 40 km/h to 30 km/h in the Carlington West area; 

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that Council approve reducing the speed limit from 40 km/h to 30 km/h in Carlington West within the area bounded by Carling Avenue to the north, Merivale Road to the east, Caldwell Avenue to the south and Clyde Avenue to the west (excluding arterial roads) and that the speed limit change be implemented through the gateway signage (traffic calming) initiative, 

    AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the costs associated with this implementation in 2027 be covered by Traffic Services, as part of its revised Speed Zoning Policy or if that is deferred and/or not implemented in 2027, the River Ward (Ward 16) traffic calming budget.
