7.22
Item 13.1 Aris Bousoulegkas, Head Coach/Director, Mississauga Aquatic Club and Andrea Pittis, General Manager

 

A. Bousoulegkas spoke to the petition requesting the inclusion of a 25-metre, 10-lane swimming pool as part of the Mississauga Valley Community Centre redevelopment.

Members of Council discussed the redevelopment project and raised questions regarding the number of Mississauga Aquatic Club members who reside in the downtown area, the feasibility and financing of a 50-metre pool, the Club’s waiting list, the public consultation process, and how the petition request would be considered through the redevelopment process. Members also enquired about the costs associated with providing pool options for Council consideration, opportunities for collaboration and volunteer involvement, whether a return on investment study had been conducted, participation by non-residents in the Club, temperature requirements for competitive swim practices, and the potential for fundraising initiatives.

A. Bousoulegkas responded to questions and provided information regarding the requirements and operations of the Mississauga Aquatic Club.

Raj Sheth, Commissioner, Community Services, spoke to the review of requests related to the Mississauga Valley Community Centre redevelopment and the development process.

Item 13.1 was brought forward for discussion.

15.2
A Notice of Motion regarding a tariff response plan and property tax relief for businesses impacted by tariffs (Mayor Parrish)

 
1.Draft Motion_Mayor Parrish_Re Tariff Relief_September 16 2026.pdf

Councillor Mahoney resumed the role of Chair at 10:17AM to 10:25AM.

Mayor Parrish spoke to the Notice of Motion, the development of a response plan, and property tax relief measures for businesses impacted by tariffs.

Members of Council discussed the inclusion of any potential funding received from senior levels of government in the report and requested further information regarding the communication and implementation process. Marisa Chiu, Commissioner, Corporate Services, provided information regarding the tax bill and eligibility requirements for the proposed program.

Jason Bevan, Director, City Planning Strategies, outlined the engagement strategy involving the Mississauga Board of Trade (MBOT) and Economic Development.

Direction was given to the Mayor to send a letter to the federal and provincial governments, as well as Mississauga Members of Parliament (MPs) and Members of Provincial Parliament (MPPs), in support of Economic Development's funding application.

 

  • RESOLUTION0182-2026
    Moved ByMayor C. Parrish
    Seconded ByDeputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac

    WHEREAS the suspension of trade negotiations and escalation of US tariff policies against Canada present a significant and ongoing threat to Mississauga’s businesses and workers; 

    AND WHEREAS on August 25, 2026, Mayor Carolyn Parrish met with the President and CEO of the Mississauga Board of Trade, the City’s Acting Chief Administrative Officer and the City’s Director of Economic Development to map out ways the City can advocate for and support small and medium-sized businesses and jobs disrupted by uncertainty; 

    AND WHEREAS Council approved Invest Mississauga's Partners in Trade Response Plan in March 2025, establishing a city-wide response to support small and medium-sized businesses through advisory services, export diversification, access to government programs, advocacy, and procurement measures that strengthen domestic suppliers and local supply chains;

    AND WHEREAS the City has already taken tariff-related actions in the City’s procurement framework to support Ontario-based suppliers and strengthen local supply chains;  

    AND WHEREAS City staff has already applied for $1.5 million in funding under the federal Regional Tariff Response Initiative (RTRI), as securing this funding would help us deliver a trade mission acceleration program designed to help Mississauga’s small and medium-sized businesses diversify into non-US markets through export readiness training and trade missions;

    AND WHEREAS the City has already established property tax rebate and assistance programs for seniors who: (a) receive the federal guaranteed income supplement (GIS) or disability tax credit under the Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP), as provided under the City’s Tax Rebate By-law 0056-2010; (b) qualify under the property taxation extreme poverty or sickness relief program under section 357 of the Municipal Act;

    AND WHEREAS a property tax deferral program is a potential tax-based financial relief mechanism for further evaluation as a means of providing financial support to businesses experiencing financial hardship due to tariff-related economic pressures;
    AND WHEREAS the 2026 tax instalment dates for non-residential properties have already occurred, any tax relief measures would be for the 2027 tax year;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT: 

    1.    The Commissioner of Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer, in consultation with the Commissioner of Legislative Services & City Solicitor and Commissioner of Planning and Building, be directed to evaluate options and report back to Council at a later date on a tariff response plan and property tax relief for businesses impacted by tariffs, including, but not limited to:

    (a)    broad property class or general tax payment relief for the 2027 tax year;

    (b)    property tax deferral for businesses experiencing financial hardship due to tariffs;

    (c)    an application-based tax instalment deferral program;

    (d)    community investment initiatives;

    (e)    economic development initiatives;

    (f)    grant funding opportunities;

    (g)    planning and development measures: Community Improvement Plan (CIP) for commercial and industrial properties, and Development Charges; and

    (h)    procurement initiatives.

     

    YES (11)Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney, Deputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac , Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor N. Hart, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor S. McFadden, and Councillor B. Butt
    Carried (11 to 0)

15.4
A Notice of Motion to Strengthen Tobacco and Vapour Product Regulation and Youth Protection in Mississauga (Councillor Damerla)

 
1.Motion to Strengthen Tobacco and Vapour Product Regulation and Youth Protection in Mississauga.pdf

Councillors Damerla and Reid spoke in support of the Notice of Motion and to the work being done by the Region of Peel. 

  • RESOLUTION0184-2026
    Moved ByCouncillor D. Damerla
    Seconded ByDeputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac

    WHEREAS tobacco and vapour products pose significant health risks, particularly to youth, and their sale, supply, display and promotion are regulated under provincial and federal legislation;
    AND WHEREAS Peel Public Health is responsible for local inspection and enforcement of the Smoke-Free Ontario Act, 2017, including requirements intended to prevent the sale and supply of tobacco and vapour products to persons under 19;
    AND WHEREAS the City of Mississauga currently requires businesses selling tobacco, cigars or cigarettes to be licensed under Business Licensing By-law 0001-2006, but the existing licensing framework may not fully reflect the evolving range of tobacco, vapour and related nicotine products available in the marketplace;
    AND WHEREAS municipal business licensing can complement provincial and federal regulation by identifying businesses and premises, establishing local operating requirements, supporting inspections and referrals, maintaining compliance records, and providing licensing consequences where municipal requirements are not met;
    AND WHEREAS Council wishes to strengthen the City’s regulatory framework, use all lawful municipal tools available to protect youth from harm, and support coordinated enforcement among the City, Peel Public Health and other authorities having jurisdiction;
    NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT:
    1. Council direct the Commissioner of Community Services, in consultation with the City Commissioner of Legislative Services and City Solicitor and other appropriate City staff, to review the City’s existing licensing and regulatory framework for businesses involved in the sale or distribution of tobacco, vapour and related nicotine products.
    2. Staff consult with Peel Public Health and other appropriate stakeholders and report back on options to strengthen municipal regulation through business licensing, including:
    a. appropriate licence categories and requirements for businesses selling tobacco, vapour or related nicotine products;
    b. measures intended to protect youth and promote compliance with applicable laws;
    c. the licensing of operators and premises and the maintenance of accurate licensing and compliance records;
    d. inspection, follow-up and progressive licensing consequences for serious or repeated non-compliance;
    e. a cost-recovery approach to licence, renewal, inspection and reinspection fees; and
    f. education, communication and implementation measures for businesses and the public.
    3. The Commissioner of Legislative Services and City Solicitor advise on the City’s authority to license and regulate businesses and operations associated with tobacco, vapour and related nicotine products, including retail, warehouse, storage, fulfilment, online-order and delivery or dispatch operations located within Mississauga.
    4. Staff consider a coordinated referral and information-sharing protocol, where legally permissible, so suspected contraventions identified through municipal licensing or inspection activity may be referred to Peel Public Health, Health Canada, the Canada Revenue Agency, law enforcement or another authority having jurisdiction.
    5. The report back identify any required amendments to the Business Licensing By-law, Licensing Administrative Penalty By-law, Fees and Charges By-law or other City by-laws, together with implementation costs, consultation requirements, transition provisions and a recommended implementation timetable.
    6. Staff assess appropriate measures for monitoring and publicly reporting on the effectiveness of the municipal licensing framework, including licensing, inspection, compliance, referral and enforcement outcomes.
    7. Staff identify opportunities to communicate recurring compliance concerns, emerging products and regulatory gaps to the appropriate provincial or federal authorities to support broader youth-protection and public-health objectives.

    YES (11)Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney, Deputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac , Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor N. Hart, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor S. McFadden, and Councillor B. Butt
    Carried (11 to 0)
